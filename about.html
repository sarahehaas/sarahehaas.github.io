About

I work where complexity meets the person trying to make sense of it.

My background is in journalism, but the throughline is broader: finding the signal, shaping the system, and helping people understand what matters, why it matters, and what to do next.

Content designer by trade. Systems thinker by nature. Journalist by training. People person by heart.

Hi, I'm Sarah.

I've spent 20 years making sense of complexity — newsrooms first, then mobile platforms, advertiser ecosystems, and AI-assisted products. The tools and topics kept changing, but the job never did: find the signal, shape the story, and make the complex clear enough to act on.

Where I'm useful

I can adapt to almost anything, but I'm most valuable when a product, concept, team, or organization has dense information, competing needs, unclear context, or fragmented scope.

That's where I ask sharper questions, make connections and systems visible, and create language, frameworks, and workflows people can actually use — and scale.

How I think about the work

It starts with one core belief: good content is how a product explains itself. The rest follows.

  • Curiosity and questions are the sharpest tools I own. Before I write a word or map a flow, I ask: What are we actually trying to say? To whom? And why should they care?
  • The user is the hero. Content is the guide. Every flow is a story with a threshold, a struggle, and a way through. My job is getting people to the other side with the thing they came for.
  • Learn the rules like a pro. I came up on AP style and newspaper deadlines, where being clear and being right were the same job. I break the rules now — on purpose, and I can tell you why.
  • Confusion is a bug. Jargon, vagueness, flows that don't quite add up — I treat them as defects, not style choices. Clarity is a form of respect.
  • Simple, but significant. Reduction isn't the goal. Meaning per word is.

A few true things

  • I co-founded an animal rescue and have fostered more than 500 dogs and cats. Chaos management is a transferable skill.
  • I also co-founded a theater company, and I write plays. Storytelling isn't a metaphor I use about design — it's a thing I practice on purpose.
  • Accessibility is a standard, not a checklist. I've championed it from grassroots advocacy to org-wide product strategy — and it's one of my favorite case studies.
  • I'm less serious in person. The rigor is real. So is the laugh.
  • Ask me about pop culture, social psychology, or where to find a good glass of wine.

My approach

How I turn content into impact

01

Understand the system

Map the ecosystem, surface the evidence, and identify what is actually creating confusion or friction.

02

Find the decision

Clarify what people need to understand, what the product needs to accomplish, and where those needs collide.

03

Design the explanation

Use language, structure, interaction, and visual hierarchy to make the product’s logic legible.

04

Make quality reusable

Turn successful decisions into frameworks, standards, workflows, or source-of-truth systems that scale.

User empathy is one of the hardest things to protect. I hold space for the human experience and show why clarity serves the bottom line.

People don't confidently use, trust, or act on information or within a system they don't understand. That is why I treat UX as a core product outcome and build clarity into development from the start.
