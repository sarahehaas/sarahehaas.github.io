Designing continuity into an in-app browser

People often left Meta’s in-app browser before they were finished exploring. Returning could mean retracing their steps, finding the original link again, or losing the context entirely. I helped define what continuity should mean for the browser — turning an ambiguous return problem into a product model, investment case, and launched Android MVP.

Problem

Returning was fragmented, but the underlying need wasn’t clearly defined.

The team knew people came back to browser content, but continuing something unfinished was different from revisiting something viewed earlier.

Outcome

A continuity model became a funded, launched product.

I helped define the user need, product model, and strategic narrative that moved Tabs from exploration to a funded roadmap commitment and Android MVP.

The challenge

The team needed to prove that returning to browser content was a distinct product problem — not simply another form of engagement.

People return to browser content for different reasons. Sometimes they want to revisit something from the past. Other times, they are still actively exploring and simply need to pick up where they left off.

That distinction mattered. Link History supported revisitation; Tabs needed to support continuation.

The approach

I turned an ambiguous return problem into a product strategy.

01

I defined the continuity problem around what people were trying to accomplish.

I synthesized research and product behavior into a model of how people move through nonlinear journeys: discovering, reconsidering, revisiting, and continuing.

That reframed Tabs from “another browser feature” into a specific job: preserve active browsing context so people can continue without starting over.

02

I turned the strategy into a product model teams could build.

I helped define how Tabs should behave across entry, return, selection, navigation, and changing browser states — including what stayed open, what appeared first, and how people resumed a session.

I also shaped the language around the experience, including first-use education and naming, so the model stayed clear without adding unnecessary friction.

03

I built the investment story and adapted the model through launch.

A strong product model still needed a reason to fund it. I connected the continuity problem to user value and browser strategy, building the narrative leadership needed to evaluate the opportunity and commit roadmap investment.

As experiments changed the implementation, I helped preserve the core continuity model while adapting the experience toward an Android MVP, iOS testing, and early Instagram exploration.

Impact

The concept became a funded product — and exceeded its engagement target.

Tabs moved from an ambiguous continuity opportunity to a funded roadmap commitment and launched Android MVP.

The work also gave the team a clearer model for supporting continuation without conflating it with browsing history.

Reflection

Good product strategy does not guarantee that a feature will survive.

Tabs was eventually deprecated, but the underlying continuity need was real.

The durable value of the work was defining the problem clearly enough to build, test, learn, and make a better product decision — rather than treating engagement itself as the strategy.
