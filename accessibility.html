Meta · Accessibility readiness

Making a complex product ecosystem testable and fixable

An accessibility program that began by mapping the product ecosystem and building the operational conditions required for reliable testing and remediation.

Role, scope & contribution

My role

Content Design accessibility lead and cross-functional program partner.

My contribution

I helped define scope, map the ecosystem, prepare vendor-ready test paths and accounts, prioritize findings, assign ownership, coordinate fixes, and shape the longer-term accessibility strategy.

Challenge

The product was too complex to audit reliably without first making it navigable.

Accessibility vendors could not simply open the product and discover every relevant state. The ecosystem included conditional flows, multiple entry points, account states, text, icons, and hidden dependencies. Testing quality depended on creating a complete, repeatable view of what needed to be tested.

Approach

Create the testable system before running the test.

Inventory

Audited every flow and cataloged states, strings, icons, and interaction paths.

Vendor enablement

Built test accounts, navigation instructions, workflow documentation, and clearly bounded scope.

Issue operations

Translated vendor scores and findings into priorities, owners, and coordinated remediation work.

Closed loop

Re-ran audits, tracked fixes, and used the work to inform a durable accessibility strategy.

Impact 1

The audit became more complete and actionable.

Vendors received a structured environment rather than an open-ended product. That made coverage more reliable and findings easier to trace back to specific flows, components, content, and owners.

Impact 2

Accessibility shifted from a one-time audit to an operating model.

The work connected testing, prioritization, ownership, remediation, and re-auditing. It also exposed where teams needed better component guidance, workflow standards, and earlier accessibility checks.

Evidence & examples

Accessibility strategyFrom immediate fixes to long-term standards and earlier quality gates.

Reflection

Accessibility work is systems work.

The visible output may be a list of issues, but the deeper design challenge is creating the product map, ownership model, and operational process that allow those issues to be found and fixed.
