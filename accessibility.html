Making a complex product ecosystem testable and fixable

Accessibility findings already existed, but the browser lacked a durable way to turn them into ongoing product work. I co-drove the cross-functional program: triaging unresolved findings, mapping roughly 360 Facebook and Instagram IAB flow and platform variations, preparing reproducible test conditions for a new audit, and shaping the ownership and remediation model that helped inform the broader organizational response to accessibility readiness.

Problem

Prior findings existed, but there was no durable product process for ownership, remediation, or reassessment.

Outcome

A repeatable operating model connected prior findings, a new baseline, clear ownership, remediation, and audit-ready setup—and helped shape a broader organizational accessibility response.

My role

As the accessibility subject-matter expert, I co-drove the program, triaged prior findings, identified priority flows, and partnered with Design and Engineering to make the ecosystem reproducibly testable.

Key decision

Use limited audit capacity on foundational blockers and representative priority flows rather than treating roughly 360 flow and platform variations as equally urgent.

The program helped secure leadership sponsorship, roadmap priority, and resources for accessibility work, and contributed to the broader waves model used to organize accessibility remediation across the organization. As part of the companywide EAA-readiness effort, this work contributed to mitigating billions of dollars in potential legal and regulatory risk.

The challenge

The findings weren’t the hardest part. Creating the conditions to act on them was.

An earlier accessibility assessment had produced findings roughly two years before, but they had not been systematically remediated. I revisited the prior triage notes and organized issues by relevance, severity, implementation effort, and ownership so the team could focus first on foundational blockers.

At the same time, we needed a new baseline. The browser contained hundreds of conditional paths across Facebook and Instagram, iOS and Android. With a limited audit budget, we had to map the larger ecosystem, select a comparable set of priority flows, and make those experiences reproducible for outside testers.

What I did

I turned fragmented accessibility work into a program the team could run.

I led the scope and operating model as an accessibility SME. I aggregated prior findings, identified foundational priorities, facilitated ownership across Content Design and Engineering, and tracked targeted fixes.

To prepare the new audit, I partnered with Product Design and teammates to identify roughly 360 flow and platform variations. I created the test setup—accounts, seeded content, product states, and step-by-step paths—then validated the selected experiences we packaged with Central Accessibility for external testing.

While the audit was underway, I co-developed an EAA Design Response Team with Content Design, Product Design, and accessibility leads. I used the browser approach to help shape an org-level proposal, leadership prioritization and resourcing, and the broader “waves” model for addressing accessibility in manageable increments.

Resolution

Accessibility became an operating model, not a one-time audit.

The work connected prior findings to ownership and remediation, established a new baseline, and created repeatable conditions for assessment. The browser approach also helped inform a broader organizational response as teams prepared for EAA readiness.

Reflection

The durable work wasn’t the audit. It was making accessibility operable.

An audit can identify problems once. A program creates the conditions to keep finding, prioritizing, owning, fixing, and reassessing them. That shift—from an assessment event to repeatable product practice—is what makes accessibility scale.
