Redesigning the content-to-code handoff with AI

Content Design finalized product strings, then handed them to Engineering for manual implementation—a series of handoffs that added delay, transcription risk, and rework. Because the work supported highly regulated, compliance-sensitive product experiences, implementation accuracy and engineering safeguards were critical. I designed and piloted an AI-assisted, human-in-the-loop workflow that let content designers add strings directly to code while Engineering retained technical review and production controls.

Problem

The handoffs created unnecessary delay, errors, and rework in a process where implementation accuracy was critical.

Outcome

Content Design gained greater implementation autonomy while Engineering retained technical review and production safeguards—and could focus its time on higher-priority engineering work.

The challenge

Every handoff introduced delay and risk.

Strings moved from source-of-truth copy to Content Design, then to Engineering for manual implementation in code. Each transfer created opportunities for transcription errors, content drift, and rework—especially costly in regulated, compliance-sensitive experiences.

The opportunity was to move implementation closer to content expertise without weakening Engineering review, source-of-truth validation, or production safeguards.

What I did

I redesigned ownership around expertise.

Content Design owned the language and implementation. AI supported structured code changes and validation. Engineering retained technical review, code quality, and production integration.

I defined the prompt logic, workflow requirements, source-of-truth checks, metadata, conditional logic, and validation steps that allowed content designers to prepare and implement strings safely. I also created a focused review experience so Engineering could inspect only the relevant content changes instead of sorting through unrelated code.

The goal wasn’t to blur roles. It was to give each discipline greater control over the work it understood best.

Resolution

The workflow increased Content Design autonomy without removing Engineering safeguards.

A live product pilot demonstrated that Content Design could own more of implementation while Engineering focused on technical review, code quality, and production integration. The redesigned workflow reduced transcription risk and gave both disciplines a narrower, more relevant review surface.

Reflection

AI made the workflow faster—and made more issues preventable.

AI let me build variables, metadata, conditional logic, standards validation, and technical constraints into the workflow so issues could be identified and corrected before strings ever reached the codebase. Human review remained essential, but it started from a cleaner, more complete implementation. That expanded Content Design’s autonomy and let Engineering focus its review on technical integrity and production risk.
