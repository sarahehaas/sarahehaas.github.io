I redesign workflows

Creating an AI-first model for content designers to implement in code

Traditional content implementation depended on a chain of handoffs between Content Design and Engineering. Those handoffs consumed time, introduced avoidable errors, and pulled both disciplines into work that didn’t always make the best use of their expertise. I designed a new AI-first operating model from scratch: content designers could implement approved content directly in code, with Engineering review and production safeguards built into the workflow.

Problem

Approved content moved through multiple people and systems before reaching production, creating opportunities for errors, rework, and delay at every handoff.

Outcome

In a pilot, implementation moved from five weeks to three days and eliminated eight handoffs, while Engineering retained review and production safeguards.

Featured artifact

The workflow, before and after

The challenge

The handoff, not the people, was creating preventable errors.

Approved content moved through multiple people and systems before reaching production. Each handoff created opportunities for context loss, transcription mistakes, rework, and delay.

The opportunity was to rethink the division of labor: what could Content Design own directly? What still required Engineering expertise? And where could AI remove unnecessary translation work between the two?

The approach

I created a new model for getting content into code.

01

I moved content implementation closer to the source.

With Engineering’s support, I tested a model where content designers could implement approved content directly in code using AI-assisted tools, rather than handing strings off for someone else to interpret and enter.

That gave Content Design more autonomy while reducing low-value implementation work for Engineering.

02

I redesigned ownership around expertise.

The goal wasn’t to blur roles. It was to make each role more effective.

Content Design retained ownership of language and intent. AI supported implementation. Engineering retained technical review, code quality, and production safeguards.

03

I built safeguards into the workflow.

I created the workflow, source-of-truth controls, validation steps, and review path needed to make direct implementation safe and repeatable.

That reduced opportunities for content drift while preserving the checks required before production.

Why the diff mattered

Reviewing the content became much simpler — and safer.

Before, a content change could be buried inside a dense code diff that Content Design and Engineering had to sift through together. The new workflow isolated the content change in its own diff, making it easier to understand, review, and approve.

That also reduced implementation risk: mistakes could be caught before the change reached the main codebase. If something was wrong, the specific diff could be abandoned instead of unwinding a published change.

Impact

The model improved efficiency without trading away control.

The pilot showed that content designers could take direct ownership of content implementation while preserving Engineering review and production safeguards.

It reduced elapsed time, handoffs, and manual implementation work while keeping domain ownership clear on both sides.

More importantly, it demonstrated a repeatable way to use AI to remove workflow friction without handing product judgment over to AI.

Reflection

The durable innovation wasn’t the prompt. It was a better division of labor.

AI made a different operating model possible: Content Design could stay closer to implementation, Engineering could focus on work that required engineering expertise, and both disciplines could spend less time correcting avoidable handoff errors.
