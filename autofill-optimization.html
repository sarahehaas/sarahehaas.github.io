Designing trust into Autofill

A broader product audit found that trust was a leading adoption barrier. I translated that strategic insight into clearer disclosure UX, coordinated experiments, and a reusable global content framework.

Business goal
Grow adoption without weakening trust or compliance
My contribution
Content architecture, language, research translation, review strategy
Results
Clearer baseline, reusable framework, no regression
58%shorter consent disclosure
46%fewer words across the full disclosure
0%regression in early performance signals
Challenge

Autofill delivered value, but the opt-in experience had to work harder.

  • Existing language prioritized compliance over comprehension.
  • Security and privacy concerns were not answered clearly enough.
  • Decisions were scattered across nine or more documents without a usable source of truth.
  • Regional, vault, platform, and legal variants created a complex review system.
  • Each new experiment risked reopening the same foundational decisions.
Approach

Understand the reasoning, then redesign the baseline.

I deconstructed existing legal and privacy guidance to recover why decisions had been made—not only what had previously been approved. I paired that context with user research, then built a disclosure architecture that could handle vault and non-vault experiences, US and international markets, EU/GDPR requirements, and Android and iOS formatting rules.

01

Make value clear

Explain what Autofill helps people do, not only what the company needs permission to do.

02

Use transparency to build trust

State what is saved, what is not saved, how it is protected, and where people can manage it.

03

Build for reuse

Create an approved architecture that future experiments can inherit rather than renegotiate.

Impact 1

Content optimization

The revised experience cut the consent disclosure from 63 to 26 words and reduced the full disclosure from roughly 140 to 75 words while preserving the required legal and privacy coverage. Early results showed the simpler baseline performed at parity, giving the team a cleaner foundation for testing structural and visual changes.

Before

“This information, including your CVV, will be saved and synced with Meta Pay, where it can be managed.”

After

“Tapping Save saves your info (except CVV) in Accounts Center, where you manage it.”

Impact 2

A reusable source of truth

The framework established an approved baseline across the product’s major variants and consolidated four experiment arms into one coordinated review cycle. Future teams could start from documented reasoning instead of repeating the same cross-functional negotiation.

Reflection

Clarity is an outcome even before it becomes lift.

The experiment confirmed that substantially clearer, more user-centered language could remove compliance debt without harming performance. It also made the next product question more precise: content had strengthened the baseline; the next gains would require content, structure, and visual design to work together.
