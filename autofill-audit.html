Meta · Autofill product strategy

Creating strategy when the problem wasn’t understood

A strategy audit that turned an inherited, poorly understood product ecosystem into a shared source of truth and actionable roadmap.

Impact

Role, scope & contribution

My role

Senior Content Designer and strategic partner across product design, research, data science, product management, and engineering.

My contribution

I built the evaluation framework, mapped the ecosystem, synthesized research and product evidence, identified opportunities, and translated findings into priorities teams could act on.

Challenge

The team inherited Autofill without a shared understanding of the product.

There was no single view of the end-to-end ecosystem, the most important user problems, competitive expectations, or which opportunities deserved investment. Before the team could create a credible roadmap, it needed a structured way to understand what it had inherited.

Approach

Build the understanding before prescribing the strategy.

Evaluation framework

Defined five principles and jobs-to-be-done that gave the audit consistent criteria instead of relying on subjective critique.

Ecosystem mapping

Mapped five core flows and the relationships among save, use, settings, control, trust, and recovery moments.

Triangulation

Combined experience findings with UXR, performance data, and six-competitor analysis to distinguish symptoms from root problems.

Prioritization

Synthesized five themes and 15 recommendations, separating near-term fixes from longer-term product opportunities.

Impact 1

The audit created a shared product language.

Instead of debating isolated screens, cross-functional partners could see the Autofill system, the evidence behind the findings, and the tradeoffs among opportunities. The work became a practical source of truth for planning and decision-making.

Impact 2

The findings became the plan.

The audit informed approximately 85% of H1 2026 priorities and helped the team align work across design, engineering, research, and product management. It also revealed that trust was not a side issue: 67% of users cited security and privacy concerns as the leading adoption blocker.

Reflection

Strategy begins by making the problem legible.

This work reinforced that the most valuable strategic artifact is not always the roadmap itself. Sometimes it is the shared understanding that makes a credible roadmap possible.
