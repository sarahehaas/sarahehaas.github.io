Creating Autofill strategy when the problem wasn’t understood

The team inherited a high-value product without a shared understanding of the experience, its biggest user problems, or where to invest next. I built the evaluation system that turned fragmented evidence into product direction.

Challenge

We had a product, but not a shared model of it.

The inherited experience had accumulated decisions across flows, surfaces, regions, and teams. Research, performance data, product assumptions, and prior decisions existed, but they had not been synthesized into a coherent explanation of what was working, what was blocking adoption, or what the product should become.

The goal was not simply to audit screens. It was to create enough shared understanding for the team to make defensible product decisions.
Approach

I created a framework for understanding the whole system.

  • Mapped the Autofill ecosystem across five key flows and their states.
  • Evaluated six competitors to distinguish table stakes from meaningful opportunities.
  • Triangulated product analysis with user research, product data, and existing team knowledge.
  • Created five evaluation principles and jobs-to-be-done to make judgment criteria explicit.
  • Synthesized findings into five themes and 15 recommendations.
  • Separated quick wins from longer-term strategic investments and aligned partners around priorities.
01

Make the system visible

Map relationships, states, and dependencies before optimizing individual moments.

02

Triangulate, don’t cherry-pick

Use research, data, competitive context, and product observation together.

03

Turn findings into choices

A useful audit does not end with issues; it creates principles, priorities, and a plan.

Impact 1

A shared source of truth

The audit gave product, design, research, data science, engineering, and content a common model of the experience. It made the reasoning behind priorities visible and became the reference point for subsequent product planning.

Impact 2

Strategy that changed the roadmap

The synthesis shaped approximately 85% of the following planning cycle’s priorities. It also surfaced a critical narrative thread: security and privacy concerns—not a lack of convenience—were the strongest barrier to adoption.

That finding led directly into the next chapter of the work: redesigning Autofill’s trust-sensitive consent and disclosure experience.

Reflection

Strategy starts by making ambiguity inspectable.

The creative work was not a single recommendation. It was building a way for the team to see the same system, judge it with shared principles, and make decisions from evidence rather than inherited assumptions.
